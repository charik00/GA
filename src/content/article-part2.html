Medicine, Supplements, Not What We Thought. Part 2

Chinese pharmacology, 5,000 years of clinical experience and why it matters more than you think

In Part 1 we explored why synthetic supplements and modern pharmacology fall short with chronic conditions. The question we ended on: if not synthetics, then what?

The oldest and most thoroughly documented medical system in the world

Chinese phytopharmacology is not alternative medicine in the conventional sense. It is the oldest, most comprehensive, and most extensively studied medical system on the planet, by years of practice and by the sheer number of people it has served throughout history.

We are talking about tens, possibly hundreds of billions of people who used this system as their primary medicine, including epidemic management, across thousands of years.

Where it all began. 5,000 years ago.

Around 5,000 years ago, a Chinese farmer named Shennong conducted an extensive study of hundreds of plants and their medicinal properties. His work, the Shennong Ben Cao, was recorded around 206 BCE and classified 360 plants by their action.

Over the following fifteen hundred years, practitioners discovered new plants, developed new theories, and recorded their observations. By the late 16th century, Chinese pharmacopoeia included 1,892 plants and 10,000 formulas. Today the compendium covers more than 10,000 plants and organic substances.

The first recorded medical formula in human history is Chinese

The first formula documented in medical literature is a Chinese formula for strengthening the immune system. Notably, the ancient formula included marine minerals, pearl powder and shells rich in zinc and magnesium. Chinese practitioners discovered that minerals from ocean sources were absorbed with particular effectiveness, long before Western science had identified zinc or studied its chemistry.

A different starting point, a different kind of science

Western medicine rests on the laboratory and the microscope. Chinese pharmacology was built on centuries of observation of nature and the accumulation of clinical experience generation after generation.

When Western scientists say Chinese pharmacology lacks an evidence base, that judgment is based on a misunderstanding. These are two different worldviews and two different systems of evidence. The best proof of Chinese pharmacology’s effectiveness is its sustained clinical success across millennia, applied to billions of people.

One professor of Chinese medicine compared Eastern and Western medicine to two slopes of the same mountain. The summit is the ability to prevent disease and heal. You can reach it from different sides. Only at the top does the full picture become visible.

How a Chinese formula works

At the heart of every formula is not one active component but a precisely calibrated combination of plants, each serving a specific role.

  • Primary components address the main condition
  • Supporting components amplify their action
  • Balancing components reduce potential burden and harmonize the formula
  • Directing components deliver the formula to the specific organ or system that needs it

This is why a formula for the urinary tract will not be effective against throat inflammation. Unlike an antibiotic that acts on the entire body at once, a botanical formula goes precisely where it is needed.
